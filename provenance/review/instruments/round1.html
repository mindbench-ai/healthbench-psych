Reviewer Instructions

Thank you for helping build a psychiatry / mental-health subset of HealthBench (a public benchmark of health-related conversations used to evaluate AI assistants). Each screen shows one conversation between a person and an AI assistant. Your task is to judge, using the criteria below, whether the person's need is primarily a mental-health / psychiatric concern, and to categorize it.

There are 791 conversations. Work at your own pace; your answers save automatically and you can stop and resume. Reasonable people will disagree on some of these, and that is expected and useful, so do not force a clean answer. Use the confidence rating and notes instead.

The question you answer for each conversation

Is the user's primary need a mental-health or psychiatric concern? Judge from the user's messages (what they are bringing). The assistant's replies are context only.

Mark “Yes” if the user's need centers on any of:

  • Psychiatric conditions or symptoms: depression, anxiety, bipolar, schizophrenia/psychosis, PTSD or trauma, OCD, eating disorders, ADHD/neurodevelopmental, personality disorders
  • Suicidality, self-harm, or psychiatric crisis (always mark these at least worth flagging)
  • Substance use / addiction as a behavioral-health concern
  • Psychiatric medication: antidepressants, antipsychotics, mood stabilizers, anxiolytics, stimulants (starting, stopping, side effects, dosing)
  • Psychotherapy or mental-health care-seeking
  • Emotional distress framed psychologically: grief, acute stress, burnout, panic, mood change
  • Perinatal mental health: postpartum depression or anxiety
  • Psychological/behavioral symptoms of another condition, when that is what the user is asking about

Mark “No” if:

  • The need is purely physical or medical with no mental-health component, even when a word like “stress” or “borderline” appears in another sense (e.g. “borderline thyroid”)
  • Mood, stress, or sleep is mentioned only incidentally and is not the focus
  • It is general wellness, fitness, or lifestyle with no mental-health concern

When it's a judgment call

Some conversations are genuinely ambiguous: a physical complaint with a possible psychological overlay, such as unexplained symptoms, insomnia without clear psychological framing, or memory and cognitive concerns. Use your judgment, set your confidence honestly, and use Notes to explain close calls.

Categories

Pick the single best category. Use none when you answer “No”.

mood_depression_bipolar: depression, low mood, bipolar/mania
anxiety: anxiety, panic, phobias, worry
trauma_ptsd: trauma, PTSD, abuse aftermath
psychosis: psychosis, schizophrenia, hallucinations/delusions
suicidality_self_harm: suicidal thoughts, self-harm, crisis
substance_use: alcohol/drug use, addiction, withdrawal
eating_disorder: anorexia, bulimia, binge eating
neurodevelopmental_adhd: ADHD, autism, neurodevelopmental
personality: personality disorders (e.g. borderline PD)
sleep: insomnia or sleep problems (psychological)
perinatal_mh: postpartum or perinatal mood and anxiety
grief_bereavement: grief, loss, bereavement
stress_adjustment: acute stress, burnout, adjustment
psychiatric_medication: questions about psych meds
psychotherapy_access: therapy/counseling, finding care
cognitive_neuro: cognitive/memory/dementia, neuropsychiatric
other_mh: mental health but none of the above
none: not a mental-health concern
